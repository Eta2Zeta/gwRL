# Global structural and generation audit

The stored JSON contains **407 nodes (303 land, 104 sea), 1,612 directed
connections and 806 reciprocal pairs**. Every stored record was enumerated.
The structural inventories and diagnostics in `data/` are generated by
[audit_structure.py](audit_structure.py); regional and rule findings are source-authored.
This script reads production data and writes only audit outputs.

## What passes

- Every node's ID matches its dictionary key; every neighbor target exists.
- There are no self edges or duplicate neighbor entries.
- Every connection has a reverse entry, and the tested reciprocal infrastructure
  fields agree. Directional terrain and combat river crossings are deliberately
  excluded from the symmetry requirement.
- Land IPP values are nonnegative integers. This checks representation, not whether
  the amount matches the board.
- Facility type/ID arrays have matching lengths; the facility-presence boolean
  agrees with the arrays.
- Referenced blocking seas exist. There is no gauge-change flag without a rail
  flag, or stored railway explicitly marked impassable.
- Both map generators reproduce the existing files byte-for-byte when their
  outputs are redirected to a temporary directory. See
  [rebuild_check.json](data/rebuild_check.json). Reproducibility does not establish
  source correctness.

## What fails or needs source review

| Observation | Exact scope | Implication |
|---|---|---|
| Isolated records | Cyprus; I1, I2, I8, I9, I10, I13, I14; M6, M7 | These tiles cannot be reached by any stored edge. Regional reports propose their missing neighbors. |
| Disconnected two-node component | Wake Island and P36 | Their mutual coast exists in the data, but P36 has no connection to the rest of the Pacific. |
| Sea edges using `normal` | 93 of 238 directed sea→sea edges | Normalize the representation to `sea` **after** validating whether each physical edge exists. Do not preserve a false adjacency merely to repair its terrain label. |
| Non-rulebook intrinsic terrain labels | Southern Finland, Viipuri and Karelia use `forest`; Pacific Northwest uses `forest`; Scotland uses `mountain_border` | Resolve to the glossary/board's actual intrinsic terrain; directional approaches remain separate. |
| Missing blocker seas | Five reciprocal narrow land pairs | Crossing legality cannot be evaluated faithfully until the complete blocker set is authored. |
| Misplaced narrow capacity | Kurile Islands–P9 and Kamchatka–P9 | Capacity belongs to the reviewed direct land crossing, not an ordinary coast. Preserve any real coast when removing the inappropriate attribute. |
| Unlocated naval facility | Caroline Islands' node-level major port | A port record must identify the sea access of the physical instance. |
| Facility ID collisions | Four IDs spanning 10 different land tiles in total | Deduplication and damage/ownership accounting can merge unrelated ports. |

All individual diagnostics, including the 93 sea transitions, are in
[structural_findings.json](data/structural_findings.json). A structural diagnostic
is distinct from a source-backed proposed edit.

### Facility collisions

| Reused ID | Currently shared by |
|---|---|
| `facility_somaliland_minor_port` | British, French and Italian Somaliland |
| `facility_morocco_minor_port` | French and Spanish Morocco |
| `facility_egypt_minor_port` | Lower, Upper and Western Egypt |
| `facility_africa_minor_port` | South Africa and Southwest Africa |

The four groups contain 3 + 2 + 3 + 2 = **10 different land tiles**.
These are collisions in the current data, not a verified list of real ports.
French Morocco has no printed port; WG-003 removes that claim and gives the
Spanish Morocco port its own ID. In Egypt, EA-328–331 identify a Western Egypt
major port on M8, a Lower Egypt major dockyard on M8 and a separate Lower Egypt
major port on I5. Upper Egypt has no printed port. WG-004 now lists those exact
facilities and removals instead of the misleading three-minor-port naming example.
A facility ID is an internal reference to one physical instance. Multiple coast
accesses may share it only when they belong to that same instance.

The 6 October naval review also confirms that Southwest Africa has no printed
port. WG-005 removes that unsupported claim and identifies South Africa's actual
major port on I1. The [corrected facility roster](naval_facility_reaudit.md)
reconciles physical existence, type and access before assigning an ID; a collision
diagnostic alone must never approve the facilities that happened to share an ID.

## Generator risks that explain the data defects

1. **Synthetic reverse defaults.** `link()` resets the reverse `border_terrain` to
   `normal` and `river_crossing` to false. The closing pass also creates missing
   reverse edges with those defaults. This makes a graph symmetric while leaving
   the reverse approach unaudited. It also explains many sea→sea `normal` values.
   Change the authoring API to require both directional attributes or preserve an
   explicit unknown status until each side is checked.
2. **Confidence values are discarded.** `link_rail`, `link_land` and
   `link_land_sea` accept `confidence`, but do not write it or a source reference.
   A `confidence=0.66` call is indistinguishable from a verified connection in
   the output. Replace unused numeric impressions with source-linked review status.
3. **Facility IDs discard identity.** `zone_name_part()` strips the first segment
   after `land_`. That happens to remove country prefixes on some nodes but removes
   distinguishing words such as `british`, `french`, `lower` and `western` on others.
   Generate new IDs from the complete node identity plus an instance suffix.
4. **Rail gauge is overwritten by owner.** The final loop sets `gauge_change`
   solely from a Soviet/non-Soviet owner comparison, regardless of printed gauge
   and other broad-gauge countries. Remove the heuristic after independently
   recording printed rail gauge.
5. **Generic geographic links replace printed topology.** The regional reports
   identify long-distance US rail links, invented South American rails, missing
   Soviet tiles and incorrect ocean shortcuts. Symmetry does not repair this.
6. **The source roster is incomplete.** Home-country derivation, sea ranges,
   projected campaigns and any pathfinding inherit the missing nodes. Fixing a
   few isolated records will not establish global completeness.
7. **City conversion discards terrain.** The generator converts `terrain='city'`
   to normal terrain with `is_city=True`, then special-cases only Tokyo and Hong
   Kong. Rome, Madrid and other mountain cities need independently authored
   terrain and city status. A settlement dot is not automatically a city land zone.

## Exact victory-city correction

Rulebook §3.2 p20 explicitly lists ten victory cities: Berlin, London, Paris,
Nanking, Rome, Calcutta, Moscow, Tokyo, Sydney and Stalingrad.

The current data instead marks 14 nodes. Propose changing `is_victory_city` to
false for **Denmark, Southern Finland, Warsaw, Istanbul, Kiev and Leningrad**;
change Calcutta to true; add the missing Sydney tile with true. Preserve the
eight correct existing flags. Victory-city status does not imply original
capital status, and an ordinary city can remain a city when its victory flag is
removed. Dynamic eligibility/destruction belongs to scoring/state.

## Derived Home Country errors

The separate `gw36_nation_home_country.json` is correctly separated from tile
geography, but it inherits defects from the world map. WG-038–WG-042 propose:

- Add **New England** to USA Home Country after restoring its missing mainland
  borders. The current graph-based search omits this existing mainland tile.
- Remove **South Sakhalin** from USSR Home Country after correcting its Japanese
  original roundel. Do not add it to Japan's explicit four-zone Home Country list.
- Include the missing **Northern Ireland**, **Sydney** and **19 Soviet zones**
  in their corresponding Home Country sets when their world nodes are added.

The source is the Home Country table on rulebook p6. Keep original roundel
identity distinct from current control; do not assume node prefixes establish it.

## Safe implementation order after review

1. Settle the canonical printed node roster and ambiguous identities.
2. Add missing nodes; replace false land, coast and sea adjacency; retain rejected
   alternatives as audit history, not as usable movement links.
3. Author intrinsic and directional terrain, river routes/crossings, rail and
   printed facilities from the source.
4. Add reviewed static feature layers and eliminate overloaded fields/defaults.
5. Regenerate the full-world map, home-country index and scenario projections.
6. Add regression checks tied to the source findings. Existing regional tests
   alone do not certify the world map.
