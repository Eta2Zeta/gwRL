## How it was measured

- **Pause and resume.** `3-four-games` was stopped safely at 05:39 on 27
  November (touching `STOP` and sending `SIGTERM` to supervisor 37859, which
  exited with code 0 after 18 seconds and saved `checkpoint.pt` at local network
  update 119, global 138). `evaluations-2026-11-27/models/generation-000119.pt`
  was exported from that checkpoint using `3-four-games/code`'s own
  `save_model_export`. Twelve single-core evaluation jobs (4 chunks of 6 seeds ×
  3 seats) ran from 05:41 to 06:26 at up to 8 concurrent processes via
  `evaluations-2026-11-27/run_jobs.py` and `play.sh`. At 06:26 `launch.sh`
  resumed `3-four-games` from `checkpoint.pt`, writing an identical
  `3-four-games/run/models/generation-000119.pt`.
- **Compute trained on.** The network after 138 network updates trained on 2,855
  self-play games (475 in the second part's 19 network updates and 2,380 in the
  third part's 119), across 44.8 hours of wall-clock play (9.44 hours in the
  second part and 35.37 hours in the third) and 1.10 billion network evaluations
  (244.2 million in the second part and 857.3 million in the third, from each
  part's `serving.jsonl`).
- **Failed games during training.** One game in the second part (update 18,
  `2-five-processes/run/failed-positions/game-621-seed-8038806183630416492.json`)
  and one in the third part (update 89,
  `3-four-games/run/failed-positions/game-1830-seed-3140720981921948512.json`)
  failed during search on `naval_take_casualty` with
  `FailedMoveError: Failed screening requires its actual adjacent entry sea`.
  Replaying both under `tools/replay_failed_move.py` with their respective frozen
  code trees reproduced `same_failure`. In both positions, `available` in
  `src/naval_movement.cpp` had failed to lock out the waiting continuing ship
  during the rest of Combat Movement, allowing it to sail out of the contested
  sea zone before the screening battle was fought in the Combat Phase; when the
  screening battle later failed and `continueNavalMovement` tried to retreat it
  to the second-to-last sea zone on its route, that zone was the contested sea
  zone itself rather than an adjacent entry sea
  ([naval operations](../../engine/naval.md#3-meeting-the-enemy-on-the-way),
  [Q-025](../../questions.md#open-questions)).

## What it shows and does not show

It shows that scaling the network to 256×16 and the search to KataGo's budgets
did not prevent held-out playing strength from regressing during continued
single-network self-play: between network updates 55 and 138, the Allies lost
essentially all of their earlier 34-point gain over the untrained network, the
Comintern lost about half of its gain, and the Axis stayed flat overall—even
while fresh-game value prediction and policy KL on self-play games improved
substantially.

Inferred: the Allies' regression matches the same single-opponent forgetting
mechanism observed in the earlier land-weighted run
([section 11 of the design document](../../design/README.md#why-did-training-stop-improving)).
In network updates 1 to 37, self-play Japan frequently invaded China (59%–92% of
games ended with all warlords aligned), teaching the Allies to defend against an
invading Japan. In network updates 56 to 109, Japan invaded China much less
often in self-play (25%–35%), and with a 100,000-sample replay buffer holding
only about 290 recent games (~14.5 network updates), the Allies forgot how to
defend against an early invasion—the scenario the scripted Axis always creates.

It does not show when between network updates 55 and 138 the Allies fell (saved
exports `generation-000072.pt` at update 91 and `generation-000108.pt` at update
127 have not been evaluated), whether the overnight rebound in self-play warlord
alignment (45% in updates 128 to 138) would recover the Allies on its own, or
whether the update-138 network beats the update-55 network in head-to-head play.
