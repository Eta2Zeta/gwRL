# The training run at 256×16 at its 19th network update — 25 November 2026

The training run at the sizes taken from AlphaZero and KataGo
(`studies/campaign/runs/training-256x16-2026-11-25`) started on 25 November 2026
from a fresh 256×16 network, one network for the three alliances: a quarter of
the decisions kept as training samples and searched with 800 simulations and
exploration noise, the rest searched with 150, which count the visits a reused
tree brings, no depth limit, 8 simulations a request, a network update of 50
training steps of 256 training samples after every 25 finished games, from a
replay buffer of 100,000
([design document](../../design/README.md#how-big-should-the-network-and-the-search-be)).
Its first part played 6 game processes of 5 games from 07:13 and was stopped at
07:37, over the owner's allowance of 8 cores; its second part,
`2-five-processes`, has played 5 processes of 5 games since 07:40. At 17:16,
after 19 network updates, the owner asked how the training was going, for
charts, and whether it was time to evaluate the network against the scripted
policy. This record measures the second part from a snapshot of its logs taken
then. The [evidence file](training_256x16_update_19_2026_11_25.json) holds every
number below; `report-2026-11-25/make_report.py` in the run's folder writes it
and draws the figures.

- **The run trains as built.** By 17:16 it had made 19 network updates and
  finished 496 games, 831,396 decisions, with no failed game. In its 8.74 hours
  of active play the inference server answered 7,234 network evaluations a
  second, as the speed measurements of 24 November had found for this layout
  ([record](self_play_speed_256x16_2026_11_24.md)), and the games finished 61.4
  an hour. A network update came about every 25 to 30 minutes and took 23 to 29
  seconds.
- **The supervisor stopped it six times, and the keeper started it again each
  time.** At 08:16 and 08:32 the Mac's swap had grown by 4 GB within an
  attempt. At 10:34 the run's processes reached 15 GB, the stop line under the
  allowance of 16 GB then, and at 11:21 they reached 16.3 GB and the supervisor
  killed them, so the run went on from its checkpoint of 10:34 and played the
  45 minutes in between again. After the owner raised the allowance to 20 GB,
  the run reached the new stop line of 19 GB at 15:47 and 16:21, 19.24 GB at
  the highest one-second sample. Each stop lost the games in play. The peaks
  came at the start of network updates: once a minute the run's physical
  footprints read 8 to 11 GB in the morning, outside the restarts, and 10 to 14
  GB in the afternoon, as the replay buffer filled.
- **Its CPU load rose through the day.** The median of the last ten one-minute
  windows was about 7.5 cores in the morning, 8.0 to 8.7 in the afternoon, and
  9.0 to 9.3 from 16:46, at the owner's limit: "a bit over 8 is fine, as along
  as it doesn't go to 9". The inference server's rate stayed the same, so each
  network evaluation cost the game processes more engine work as the games grew.
- **The games became much longer.** The median game had 333 decisions in the
  first network update's games, about 1,700 by the fifth and 1,800 to 2,400
  since the twelfth, the Axis making 57% to 77% of them from the second on.
  The time estimates had
  assumed the 880 decisions of the land-weighted run's trained network. At
  2,100 decisions and 61 games an hour, 145,000 games would take about 100 days
  (inferred).
- **Self-play scores have not moved much.** An alliance's final score is its
  wealth minus the other two alliances' wealth combined; the starting wealth
  gives the Axis +29.1, the Allies −45.5 and the Comintern −57.5. Over the 19
  network updates the Axis's mean final score stayed between +9.4 and +28.6,
  below its start; the Allies' between −45.6 and −27.1, above theirs; and the
  Comintern's between −88.2 and −74.7, far below its start.
- **The values predict new games no better than their mean yet.** Before a
  network update trained on its 25 new games, the network's values missed those
  games' final scores by 47.0 points with the untrained network, and by 8.6 to
  21.7 points since, with no trend. The share of the differences between the
  games that the values explained was between −0.63 and +0.36: positive from
  the 11th to the 17th network update, +0.07 to +0.36, and negative again in the
  18th and 19th, −0.21 and −0.48. With 25 games a network update, one update's
  games can differ little or much from each other, so the share swings.
- **The policy follows the search a little more closely than at first.** The KL
  divergence of the search's visit shares from the network's action
  probabilities on new games was 0.08 nats for the untrained network, whose
  searches, with values that told them little, stayed close to its priors
  (inferred), 0.59 to 0.60 at the second and third network updates, 0.46 at the
  fourth and 0.30 to 0.40 since.

## How it was measured

- **The snapshot** copies, at 17:16, the second part's `run/manifest.json`,
  `run/serving.jsonl`, `run/progress.jsonl`, `cpu_memory.log`, `keeper.log` and
  the supervisor's `state.json` of both its control folders, `run-control`
  (until 11:24) and `run-control-20gb`.
- **Each network update's games** are the 25 games of its entry in the
  manifest: their decisions, their final scores, and their fresh losses, which
  every finished game gets from the networks serving self-play before it trains
  them (`fresh_game_losses` in `studies/campaign/learning.py`). The value miss
  is 100 times the square root of the value loss, the mean over the three values
  of the squared difference between the network's values and the final scores
  divided by 100. The explained variance is one minus the value loss over the
  variance of the games' final scores divided by 100, each game weighted by its
  kept decisions and averaged over the three alliances, as
  `tools/compare_training_runs.py` works it out; the policy KL divergence is the
  fresh losses' `policy_kl` over their training samples.
- **Speed** comes from `serving.jsonl`, each invocation's lines from its fourth
  on; **load and memory** from `cpu_memory.log`, which `log_cpu_memory.py`
  writes once a minute from the processes' CPU times and `top`'s physical
  footprints; **stops** from the supervisors' events.

## What it shows and does not show

It shows that the run plays, trains and takes up its networks as designed, at
the measured speed, and that at this layout it sits at the owner's limits: its
CPU load reached 9 cores as the games grew, and its memory reached the stop line
at the start of network updates. It shows the games growing to about 2,100
decisions, which halves the games a day the estimates expected.

It does not show whether the network is learning to play better. After 19
network updates its values predict new games no better than their mean, and the
self-play scores, which depend on how all three alliances play, have not
moved beyond their spread between network updates. The network has not played
against the scripted balanced policy on the held-out seeds, which is the
measure of strength (section 10 of the design document).
