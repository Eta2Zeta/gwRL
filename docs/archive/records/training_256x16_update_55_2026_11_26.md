From the 29th network update on, the Axis's mean final score rose from between
+15 and +19 to between +25 and +33, and the Allies' fell from between −29 and
−38 to between −43 and −51, while the Comintern's stayed between −80 and −85
and the games got shorter. The owner read this as the network improving: the
Axis starts strongest, so a network that plays every alliance better lets the
Axis gain most, and since each final score subtracts the other alliances'
wealth, the Allies' score falls as the Axis's wealth grows. Self-play alone
cannot tell this apart from a network whose Allies play worse, because it
measures each alliance only against the same network. So that morning the owner
asked to play the network against the scripted balanced policy, which measures
strength ([section 10 of the design document](../../design/README.md#10-measuring-strength)).
This record gives those games. The
[evidence file](training_256x16_update_55_2026_11_26.json) holds every number
below; `make_record.py` in the run's folder `evaluations-2026-11-26` writes it
from that folder's reports and draws the figure.

- **Training has made the network better in every seat.** Against the scripted
  balanced policy in the other two seats, on the same 24 held-out seeds, the
  network after 55 network updates scored 11.3 ± 3.0 points more than the
  untrained network as the Axis, 34.0 ± 6.9 more as the Allies and 12.5 ± 2.5
  more as the Comintern.
- **Against the scripted policy it is better as the Axis and the Comintern,
  and worse as the Allies.** As the Axis it scored +21.4, 28.2 ± 5.4 points
  above the scripted balanced policy in the same seat, higher on 21 of the 24
  seeds, and ended its games with 12.4 land zones, against 8.9 for the scripted
  Axis, which invades China from game round 1, and the 11 the Axis starts with.
  The scripted Axis is weak: the untrained network, which ends almost every
  phase at once, scored 16.8 ± 5.0 points above it. As the Comintern the
  network scored 8.9 ± 3.6 points above the scripted policy, higher on 19 of
  the 24 seeds, where the untrained network scored 3.6 ± 3.1 below it. As the
  Allies it scored 32.9 ± 10.0 points below the scripted policy, higher on 6 of
  the 24 seeds: its Allies ended with 14.0 land zones, against 20.2 for the
  scripted Allies, and the scripted Axis with 12.9, against 8.9.
- **In every seat it scores above the network of the training run under the
  land-weighted score after 595 network updates**, which was evaluated on 24

  came out the same seed by seed: by 7.2 ± 2.9 points as the Axis, 39.1 ± 7.4

## How it was measured

- **When.** The training run's third part was stopped at 06:52, as its
  `launch.sh` describes, with the part's STOP file and a TERM signal to its
  supervisor, and `launch.sh` started it again from its checkpoint at 07:34.
  Meanwhile one-core processes, at most 8 at a time, played the network's 12
  jobs of six seeds and the scripted policy's 3 jobs of 24 (`play.sh` and the
  job lists in `evaluations-2026-11-26`; its `evaluations.log` says when each
  job ran). An export loads only with the sources that wrote it, so the
  untrained network's 3 jobs of 24 seeds failed at first; they then played with
  the second part's code, one at a time beside the training, from 07:34 to
  08:45, while the training's CPU load, the median of its last ten one-minute
  windows, stayed between 6.7 and 7.1 cores. All the reports name the same
  rules, scenario, actions and network input.
- **The scripted policy's games** came out the same, seed by seed and to the
  hundredth of a point, as in the evaluations of the training run under the
  land-weighted score on 24 November, in all three seats. So that run's results
  pair with these on the same seeds against the same scripted games, and its
  differences from the scripted policy stay as its record gives them
  ([record of its 595th network update](training_land_weighted_update_595_2026_11_24.md#play-against-the-scripted-balanced-policy)).
  One rule changed in between: since 24 November, Japan and Dadao never both
  attack one land zone in a turn (RCL-024, commit `c07d0f2`). The scripted
  Axis did not do so in these seeds' games; the network after 55 network
  updates plays the Axis under the rule, and the network of the training run
  under the land-weighted score did not.
- **A paired difference** subtracts two players' final scores in the same seat
  on the same seed; the tables give the mean over the 24 seeds, its standard
  error and the number of seeds on which the first player scored higher.
- **The self-play games** of a network update are those its entry in its part's
  `manifest.json` lists, the games it trained on.
- **What each network trained on.** The network after 55 network updates
  trained on the 475 games of the second part's 19 network updates and the 720
  of the third part's first 36, in about 8.7 and 13.0 hours of play and about
  540 million network evaluations of its 256×16 network (the
  [record of the 19th network update](training_256x16_update_19_2026_11_25.md)
  gives the second part's rate of 7,234 a second; the third part's
  `manifest.json` counts 313 million). The network of the training run under the
  land-weighted score after 595 network updates trained on 9,520 games in 35.4
  hours of play and 222 million network evaluations of its 256×8 network
  ([its record](training_land_weighted_update_595_2026_11_24.md)).

## What it shows and does not show

It shows that after 55 network updates and 1,195 self-play games the network
plays every seat better than its untrained network against the scripted
balanced policy, by 11.3 points as the Axis, 34.0 as the Allies and 12.5 as
the Comintern; that it plays the Axis and the Comintern better than the scripted
policy, and the Allies 32.9 points worse; and that in every seat it beats the
network of the training run under the land-weighted score after 595 network
updates, on an eighth of that network's self-play games and about 60% of its
hours of play. That run had stopped improving after 4,600 to 5,800 games, its
Axis 5 to 7 points above its untrained network and its Allies below it
([design document](../../design/README.md#why-did-training-stop-improving)).

It does not show whether the network is still improving, nor whether the
self-play rise of the Axis's score from the 29th network update came from a
stronger Axis, weaker Allies or both: this is the run's first evaluation, and
no network from before the 29th network update has played the scripted policy;
the second part's model export after 19 network updates could. It does not show
which change made the difference from the training run under the land-weighted
score: the network's size, the search behind training (800 and 150 simulations
with no depth limit, against 256 and 16 to depth 16), exploration noise at a
quarter of the decisions instead of 1 in 100, and the rule of one attacker for
each land zone changed together, and the compute was not equal: about 540
million network evaluations of a network twice as deep, against 222 million.
It does not show whether the network plays better against itself, which
self-play trains for, nor differences smaller than about twice the standard
errors above: 6 to 11 points for the Axis, 5 to 7 for the Comintern and 13
to 20 for the Allies.
