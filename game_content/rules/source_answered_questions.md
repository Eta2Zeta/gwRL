## RCL-002: influence spending before the die roll

**Status: closed after checking, 22 November 2026.** First recorded on
5 October 2026 as an interpretation the user adopted.

**Sources:** RB §15.2 p104 and §15.4 p105; CCP p3; Japan p4. The four print the
same sentences.

**Source-backed conclusion:** before rolling for influence, the CCP or Japan may
spend IPP "up to the amount of its original influence roll", and each IPP spent
subtracts 1 from the roll. The payment is made before the die is rolled, so the
words cannot mean a die result: "its original influence roll" is the number the
roll is compared with before the payment lowers it, the total number of
qualifying adjacent land zones, which a raw roll must not exceed to succeed. So
the nation spends any whole number of IPP from 0 up to that number, and no more
than its treasury holds; the attempt succeeds when the raw roll minus the IPP
spent is at most that number, except that a raw 12 always backfires to the KMT,
as printed. Eligibility, the Influencing Zone requirement and the one attempt a
turn are as printed.

Example: with 3 qualifying adjacent land zones, the nation may spend up to
3 IPP. Spending 2 makes raw rolls of 1 to 5 succeed, and a raw 12 still
backfires.

## RCL-006: when does Chinese major-power evolution trigger?

**Status: closed after checking, 6 October 2026.**

**Sources:** RB §14.3.1, p101; KMT/CCP p1; RB §10.10, p73 (possession and IPP
chart updates), §12.9, p82 (income collection).

**Source-backed conclusion:** KMT or CCP evolves immediately once the IPP values
of its possessed land zones total **13 or more**. A legal capture, alignment or
influence transfer updates possession at that event's specified time; evaluate
the threshold as that transfer resolves, including during another nation's turn.
The trigger does not wait for income collection, the end of the current turn or
the start of the faction's next turn. Treasury and income bonuses do not count
toward this possessed-land threshold.

No contradictory rule has been identified. Evolution changes the faction's
abilities and automatically terminates a Chinese Truce as specified by the
rules. The explicit exception preserves decentralized production for the
remainder of its own turn if it evolves during that turn. Neither consequence
delays evolution or repeats a phase already completed.

## RCL-007: breaking the Chinese Truce

**Status: closed after checking, 18 October 2026.**

**Source:** RB §6.6, p42.

The truce may be formed when a foreign Major Power declares war on China,
by agreement at the end of that attacker's Combat phase. It is not a freely
available prewar agreement; the foreign Major need not specifically be Japan.

**Source-backed sequence:**

1. During **Combat Movement**, the player may move some or all of its units out
   of land zones shared with the other faction, following normal movement
   restrictions and the explicit separation permission in §6.6.
2. During **Combat**, the player may break the truce.
3. Resolve combat in land zones still containing units from both factions.
   Preserve the printed KMT Low Morale consequence: eligible KMT units in shared
   zones are eliminated on rolls of 10+ when KMT breaks the truce.

The rule expressly allows separation before the formal break. **No declaration
or prior commitment to breaking the truce is required during Combat Movement.**
The player may decide to break it during Combat. Breaking the
truce in Combat does not let the player go back and redo movement or grant an
additional movement phase. This sequence does not permit attacks while the
truce is still in effect or replace ordinary combat-declaration rules.

## RCL-008: ordinary diplomacy with Chinese warlords

**Status: closed after checking, 18 October 2026. No additional clarification
or scenario ruling is needed.**

**Sources:** RB §§4.2-4.4, p26; §§4.8-4.9, p30; §8.8, p53.

**Source-backed conclusion:** ordinary diplomacy targets neutral **nations**.
The Minor Power and neutrality definitions apply to nations; a separate warlord
roundel does not make a Chinese province an independent neutral nation. Section
4.9 classifies these as semi-autonomous Chinese warlord provinces, treated as
part of KMT by outside nations. Section 4.8 explicitly distinguishes Sinkiang's
original Chinese-warlord status from territory originally possessed by a neutral
Minor Power when applying the Soviet-sphere rule.

Chinese warlords therefore are not ordinary neutral-nation diplomacy targets.
Use their specifically defined influence/alignment mechanics when legal. Their
special foreign-relations classification does not grant KMT possession, income
or asset attribution at setup. No conflicting rule has been identified; the
earlier entry overstated a source-classification question as an unresolved
interpretation. Retain the entry as a resolved reference.

## RCL-011: construction bombing, three-point setbacks

**Status: closed after checking the explicit examples, 22 October 2026.**

**Source:** RB §8.3.2 p51.

**Issue to report:** the prose says a facility under construction may take up to
three damage before further damage pushes it back. The examples require each
full three damage to cause a setback: a Major Factory at stage 2 takes five
damage and becomes stage 3 with two damage; six damage removes it entirely.
A four-point setback threshold would contradict those examples.

**Resolution:** follow both explicit examples: every full three
damage moves construction back one stage; the remainder is damage requiring
repair before construction can advance. A setback past the starting stage
removes the project. No refund or subtraction of historical paid IPP is implied.
This is an example-supported reading, not a user or publisher-issued ruling.
The wording remains useful to report to the developers.

## RCL-021: Upgrading a unit aboard a transport

**Status: closed after checking, 23 November 2026.** First recorded on
5 November 2026 as an interpretation the user adopted, and its part on ships at
sea on 6 November 2026.

**Sources:** RB §8.4 p51 (upgrades); §12.3 p80 (placing upgrades, and an
upgrade that cannot reach a suitable unit); §11.3.1 p75, §11.4.1 p76 and §11.5
pp77-78 (cargo still aboard; a ship in port); §13.1.1 p83 and §13.1.2 pp84-85
(unit classes); §13.1.5 pp88-89 and §13.1.6 p92 (what each transport carries).

**The question it answered.** An upgrade replaces one of a nation's units with a
better unit in the Place Units Phase (RB §8.4). If that unit is still aboard a
transport then, may the upgrade be placed when the transport may not carry the
upgraded unit, and may it be placed while the transport is a ship at sea?

**Source-backed conclusion:**

1. **A unit can be aboard a transport in the Place Units Phase.** An air
   transport may unload its cargo in a Friendly-possessed land zone but need not
   (RB §11.3.1). A naval transport may go in port with its cargo aboard
   (RB §11.5). A unit of an Aligned member of the transport owner's alliance
   boards on its own Player Turn, travels on the owner's and leaves only on its
   own next one (RB §§11.3.1, 11.4.1).
2. **Not aboard a ship at sea.** RB §12.3 requires a Supply Path to the land
   zone the upgraded unit is in. A ship in port is located in the land zone of
   its Naval Facility (RB §11.5, p78), and its cargo with it; a ship at sea and
   its cargo are in a sea zone, which is no land zone. So an upgrade cannot
   reach a unit aboard a ship at sea.
3. **Not into cargo the transport may not carry.** An Air Transport carries one
   infantry-class unit (RB §13.1.5, p88), a Heavy Air Transport one
   infantry-class unit, one Artillery or one Anti-aircraft Artillery (p89), and
   a Naval Transport two infantry-class units, or one infantry-class unit and
   one other land unit or Strategic Rocket (RB §13.1.6, p92). Militia and
   Infantry are infantry-class (RB §13.1.1); Motorized Infantry is vehicle-class
   (RB §13.1.2). An upgrade that would leave a transport carrying a unit it may
   not carry cannot replace that unit: an Infantry aboard an Air Transport may
   not become Motorized Infantry, and of two Infantry aboard one Naval Transport
   only one may.
4. **Such an upgrade waits.** An upgrade that cannot reach a suitable unit
   stays on the build queue until a Supply Path reaches a suitable unit in a
   later Place Units Phase (RB §12.3).

Other upgrades of carried units are allowed: an Infantry aboard a Naval
Transport in port may become Motorized Infantry when nothing else aboard is
outside the infantry class, and a Militia aboard an Air Transport may become
Infantry. No rule makes cargo leave its transport before the Place Units Phase,
so it may stay aboard; the user kept that choice open on 5 November 2026 because
staying aboard can be better, as carpet bombing attacks only land units
(RB §10.6.5, p68).

Examples: a KMT Air Transport landed in Sikang still carries an Infantry in the
KMT's Place Units Phase, and the KMT has bought an upgrade to Motorized
Infantry; the upgrade is offered only on an Infantry not aboard a transport, or
aboard one that could carry the result. A Japanese Naval Transport at sea off
Tokyo carries an Infantry and a Militia, and Japan has bought an upgrade for
each; neither is offered for them until the ship is in port or the units are
ashore.

The program has followed this reading since 6 November 2026:
`upgradeUnitLegal` (`src/economy.cpp`) offers an upgrade on a unit only if the
unit is in a land zone that a Supply Path reaches and if `upgradeKeepsCargo`
finds that its transport, if it has one, could still carry its cargo; the tests
`upgradesKeepCargo` and `upgradesWaitAtSea` (`tests/economy_tests.cpp`) check
both parts.
